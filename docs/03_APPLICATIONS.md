## Why both were rejected at screening (diagnosis, 10 Oct)
Two rejections is too small a sample to prove the CV is bad. The likely causes, most to least likely:
1. **Hard filters.** PayPay asks 4+ years of Java (you have 3+). Tipico (Germany) means visa sponsorship and relocation for a mid-level role where local/EU applicants exist. ATS and recruiters drop these first.
2. **No public evidence.** No GitHub, no project links. Abroad screeners can't check BJIT's work, so the CV is all words.
3. **Only stretch roles.** Both were top-choice abroad roles. There were no "good match" roles to balance them.
4. **No referral, cold apply.** For abroad roles, referrals raise the chance of a callback many times over.
5. **Keyword fit.** The generic EU CV may miss JD words (e.g. "payments", "Kafka", "Kubernetes", "event-driven", "AWS").

## Fixes (apply from 12 Oct)
- **Volume and mix:** 8–10 per week, about 70% good-match roles (3–5 yrs, Java/Spring, open to sponsorship or BD/remote) and 30% stretch roles.
- **Speed:** apply within 48 h of the posting date. Older posts get far fewer callbacks.
- **Referral first** for every top target: send 1–2 LinkedIn notes (Outreach Kit templates) to engineers there, *then* apply.
- **Tailor in 10 minutes:** paste the JD into Claude (prompt C5). Mirror the top 6 keywords in the summary and top 3 bullets. Never add skills you can't defend.
- **Proof:** add a CV "Projects" section with the GitHub link once ledger-service M2 is public (target Sun 25 Oct). The repo itself goes public on Fri 16 Oct. Add the same link to LinkedIn Featured.
- **Filter honestly:** skip roles with "German/Japanese required (business level)" or "5+ yrs" unless you have a referral.
- **Checkpoint:** after 20 applications, if callbacks are under 2, Claude reviews the CV again using the actual JDs.
